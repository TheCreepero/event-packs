## salengro_affair
Used: 2026-11-01-france-first-events.md
Date: November 1936 (suicide in the night of 17 to 18 November; funeral 22 November)
Kind: politics and society (press scandal)
Suggested window: 10 November 1936 to 30 November 1936, at peace
Facts:
- Roger Salengro, Minister of the Interior in the Blum government, killed himself in Lille after a far-right press campaign accused him of desertion in the First World War. FR36 gives 17 November, other sources 18 November. Source: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936_en_France (1936 en France)
- The campaign was run mainly by the papers Gringoire (director Henri Béraud) and L'Action française. Source: https://www.herodote.net/almanach-ID-2896.php (17 novembre 1936) [via search summary]
- A war commission cleared him on 13 November 1936; the Chamber rejected the accusations by a large majority. Source: https://fr.vikidia.org/wiki/Roger_Salengro (Roger Salengro) [via search summary; Vikidia is a weak source, trust the commission date less]
- He left a letter to Léon Blum and one to his brother; the funeral was in Lille on 22 November 1936. Source: https://kartable.fr/ressources/histoire/personnages/la-republique-et-la-question-ouvriere-le-front-populaire-2/5820 (Kartable, Roger Salengro) [via search summary]
- A law toughening penalties for press defamation was voted on 18 December 1936 (per Vikidia). Source: https://fr.vikidia.org/wiki/Roger_Salengro (Roger Salengro)
Anchors: Roger Salengro; Lille; Gringoire; Henri Béraud; 13 November 1936; 22 November 1936; Léon Blum
Choice: How to answer the press: defend the minister's honour in the Chamber, prosecute the newspapers, mourn with a state funeral, or let the press stay free.
Picture ideas: Crowd following the coffin in the streets of Lille; a Gringoire front page of 1936.

- Jean Dehaene, executed 19 July 1939 at Saint-Brieuc, was the first guillotined inside a prison (per Wikipedia). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_punishment_in_France (Capital punishment in France)
- Press behaviour and crowd size: UNVERIFIED. Avoid graphic detail in the text.
Anchors: Eugen Weidmann; Versailles; prison Saint-Pierre; 17 June 1939; 24 June 1939
Choice: Ban public executions at once, keep the spectacle as a deterrent, or ban the press photographers only.
Picture ideas: The Versailles street outside the prison in the pre-dawn crowd (no execution image).

## marseille_nouvelles_galeries_fire

Kind: politics and society (disaster)

Facts:

Source: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/1938_en_France (1938 en France) and https://madeinmarseille.net/24115-incendie-nouvelle-galerie-canebiere/ (incendie nouvelle galerie canebiere) [via search summary]

- The city was put under state tutelage and the Bataillon de marins-pompiers de Marseille was created by decree-law of 29 July 1939 (one source says 23 July). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marseille_Naval_Fire_Battalion (Marseille Naval Fire Battalion)
- The same day the Radical Party congress met in Marseille (FR38). Source: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/1938_en_France (1938 en France)
Anchors: Nouvelles Galeries; la Canebière; 28 October 1938; 73 morts; marins-pompiers de Toulon; Amiral Muselier
Choice: Fund a military fire battalion, blame the store owners with new safety rules, or leave local control as it was.
Picture ideas: Smoke over the Canebière and ladders on the department store facade.

## rhineland_march_1936
Date: 7 March 1936 and the following days
Kind: military and defence (diplomacy)
Suggested window: 7 March 1936 to 25 March 1936
Facts:
- 7 March 1936: Germany remilitarised the Rhineland; France referred the matter to the League of Nations; Hitler proposed 25-year non-aggression pacts. Source: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936_en_France (1936 en France)
- Premier Albert Sarraut and Foreign Minister Pierre-Étienne Flandin favoured a firm answer; General Gamelin warned about risks; thirteen divisions went into defensive positions near the border; Flandin was sent to London on 11 March. Source: https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/history/german-troops-march-rhineland (German Troops March into the Rhineland) [via search summary]
- General Louis Maurin proposed a partial, temporary show of force in the Council of Ministers; a general mobilisation six weeks before elections was rejected and no measure was taken (per Flandin). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Maurin (Louis Maurin)
- 17 March 1936: active military service extended to two years (FR36). 8 April: Germany rejected the French peace plan. Source: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936_en_France (1936 en France)
- The sentence about Strasbourg under German guns attributed to Sarraut is reported without a primary source: UNVERIFIED, do not quote.
Anchors: Albert Sarraut; Pierre-Étienne Flandin; Général Gamelin; 7 mars 1936; treize divisions; Société des Nations
Choice: Mobilise, act through the League, or consult London first.
Picture ideas: French reservists or cavalry on the Lorraine border in March 1936.

Anchors: 10 milliards de francs; 4 %; 60 ans; 16 March 1937
Choice: Appeal to small savers with posters, lean on the banks, or add a compulsory levy.
