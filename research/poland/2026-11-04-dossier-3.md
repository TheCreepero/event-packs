## Conventions
- Fact line: `Source: URL (title). Quote: «...»`. Quotes are the lines the fetch returned from the page that was opened. The fetch tool sometimes condenses a page, so treat quotes marked (condensed) as to be re-checked by the checker.

- Excluded subjects avoided: Anglo-Polish guarantee, Danzig politics, COP, Sanation/OZN, Zaolzie, etc.
- Web calls used: about 24 of the ~40 budget. Several Wikipedia URLs returned 404 (Pyry conference, Polish gold evacuation, FIS 1939 sources fine).

## mobilisation_postponed
Date: 29-31 August 1939
Kind: military / diplomacy
Suggested window: 28 Aug - 1 Sep 1939
Facts:
- 29 Aug 1939 general mobilisation was ordered, then cancelled under British and French pressure; reissued 30 Aug to take effect 31 Aug. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_invasion_of_Poland (Timeline of the 1939 invasion of Poland). Quote: «The Polish government orders general mobilization of the military again, to go into effect on 31 August.»
- Beck on 30 Aug (via Ambassador Biddle). Source: https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1939v01/d387 (FRUS 1939 vol. 1, doc. 387, Biddle to Secretary of State, 30 Aug 1939, 5 p.m.). Quote: «while last night's postponement of general mobilization went deeply against the grain» and «either or both London and Paris could count upon Poland as an ally under any circumstances»
- Sources differ on how far the delay was Allied pressure versus Poland's own decision (forum citing Weinberg: [via search summary] UNVERIFIED). Trust the FRUS telegram.
Anchors: Józef Beck; Edward Rydz-Śmigły; 29 August; 31 August; London and Paris
Choice: Postpone mobilisation to please the Allies, or call it at once and risk a charge of provocation.
Picture ideas: Mobilisation posters (red, white); reservists at a railway station.

- Signal "Execute Peking" received 12:55, departure 14:15. The page dates this 29 Aug in one line. Same page.
- Ships: ORP Burza (Stanisław Nahorski), Błyskawica (Włodzimierz Kodrębski), Grom (Aleksander Hulewicz); group led by Roman Stankiewicz. They docked at Leith 1 Sep 17:37. Same page.
- German cruisers Nürnberg, Köln, Leipzig were recalled to the Baltic. Same page.
- Grom sunk 4 May 1940 off Narvik. Same page (post-period, do not use).
- Date disagreement: the page text gives 29 Aug as the day the signal was received and 30 Aug as the German cruiser recall; check the exact 30 Aug date before use.
Anchors: ORP Burza; ORP Błyskawica; ORP Grom; Józef Unrug; Leith; Øresund
Choice: Keep the destroyers in the Baltic for local defence, or send them to Britain.

- Garrison: older sources 182 men; recent research 210-240. Same page.
- Schleswig-Holstein opened fire about 04:45 (sources vary 04:43-04:48, some 04:00). Same page, and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_invasion_of_Poland. Quote: «opens fire on the Polish garrison at Westerplatte, Free City of Danzig, starting the battle of Westerplatte»
- Staff Sgt Wojciech Najsarek killed about 05:15. Timeline page.
- Surrender 7 Sep; white flag 09:45; Sucharski surrendered to Capt. Gustav Kleikamp. Battle page.
- Polish Radio slogan. Battle page. Quote: «Westerplatte broni się jeszcze» ("Westerplatte fights on").
Anchors: Westerplatte; Henryk Sucharski; Franciszek Dąbrowski; Schleswig-Holstein; 1 September 1939; 04:45
Choice: Hold to the last man, or order withdrawal and surrender.
Picture ideas: The Westerplatte guardhouses; Schleswig-Holstein firing.
Note: subject is the military battle, not Danzig politics.
